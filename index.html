Tactile-JEPA: Topology-Aware Self-Supervised Representation Learning for Distributed Tactile Sensors

Anonymous authors

CodeMethodInteractive masksResults

Abstract

Distributed electronic skins produce signals on sparse, irregular sensor layouts. Tactile-JEPA uses this spatial structure for self-supervised representation learning. It predicts the embeddings of masked sensing elements from visible context, with masks sampled over a sensor connectivity graph. Local targets cover connected regions; global targets select sensors distributed across the surface. The default strategy combines two of each. We evaluate frozen representations on Sparsh-skin, Tactile Socks and DECO-50, spanning force and pose estimation, classification and visuo-tactile action prediction. Tactile-JEPA reduces force estimation RMSE by 6.3% and in-hand orientation RMSE by 20.8% relative to the strongest baseline for each metric. Performance depends on the downstream task: MAE achieves the lowest policy error on DECO-50.

Method

Each sensor’s short signal history is projected to a token. A context encoder processes visible tokens, and a predictor estimates target embeddings. These are matched with the corresponding outputs of an exponential-moving-average target encoder, which receives the complete input.

During downstream evaluation, the pre-trained tactile encoder is frozen and a task-specific head is trained on top. The graph guides mask sampling during pre-training; it is not required as a downstream input. The objective predicts embeddings rather than reconstructing sensor readings.

Datasets

We study magnetic and piezoresistive sensing across single-hand, bipedal and bimanual configurations. DECO-50 groups 2,124 raw taxels into 528 training nodes.

About these visualizations and results +

Tactile-JEPA: Topology-Aware Self-Supervised Representation Learning for Distributed Tactile Sensors. This research companion uses the supplied Tactile-JEPA manuscript for metrics and repository assets for geometry. Error values are shown with the reported ± spread; they are not confidence intervals. “Collapsed” denotes reported training collapse, not a score of zero.

Sparsh-skin uses a saved 368-taxel hand geometry and the physical graph builder (bridge_k = 4). Tactile Socks uses the exported 453-sensor physical map and four-neighbour graph; the right foot is mirrored for side-by-side display. DECO uses the exact coarse graph from deco_geometry.py: 264 nodes per hand. Its planar positions describe a canonical layout, not measured 3D anatomy. The optional 3D Socks and DECO views are illustrative deformations of these maps; the flat Sparsh-skin view is a projection of its saved 3D geometry. Both layouts preserve the same graph and masks. The manuscript uses fixed sensor adjacency for mask sampling, not the display coordinates.

G denotes global random targets (R in experiment identifiers). Mask samples use the repository’s Dijkstra sampler, with fixed illustrative budgets and seeds. Connected context is sampled within each part of paired sensors and can fragment after target removal. This explorer is not a replay of a training batch; alternative controls do not imply an evaluated experiment.

Recorded episodes and model predictions are not bundled in this version. No synthetic contact signals or robot rollouts are presented as experimental results.
